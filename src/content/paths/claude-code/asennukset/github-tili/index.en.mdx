On <Term id="github">GitHub</Term>, your projects are kept in the cloud, and later you can publish your site from there. The account is free.

## 1. Create an account

1. Go to [github.com/signup](https://github.com/signup).
2. Enter an email address, a password and a username. Choose an email address you want to keep using.
3. Confirm your email address with the message GitHub sends you.

<Tip title="Choose your username carefully">

Your username is public, and it also becomes part of your published site’s address (e.g. `alex-smith.github.io`). A short and clear name is best.

</Tip>

<NuppuSays pose="suojaa">Two-factor authentication is like having a second lock on the door. Setting it up takes a moment, but it protects your account for a long time.</NuppuSays>

## 2. Turn on two-factor authentication

<Term id="kaksivaiheinen-tunnistautuminen">Two-factor authentication</Term> means that signing in requires your phone in addition to your password. Even if someone found out your password, they couldn’t get into your account. GitHub strongly recommends it and may also require it later. If you turn it on right away, it’s taken care of.

1. Install an authenticator app on your phone, for example Microsoft Authenticator or Google Authenticator.
2. On GitHub, open **Settings** → **Password and authentication** from your profile picture in the top-right corner.
3. Choose to enable two-factor authentication and choose the app option (Authenticator app). Scan the QR code on the screen with the app and type the code the app shows into GitHub.
4. **Save the recovery codes** in a safe place, for example print them or save them in a password manager. They get you into your account if you lose your phone.

Source: [GitHub’s instructions for two-factor authentication](https://docs.github.com/en/authentication/securing-your-account-with-two-factor-authentication-2fa/configuring-two-factor-authentication).

<ReadMore title="Why isn’t a text message the best option?">

GitHub also supports codes by text message, but it primarily recommends an authenticator app. In the worst case, text messages can be hijacked, for example through a scam where your phone number is moved to another SIM card.

</ReadMore>
