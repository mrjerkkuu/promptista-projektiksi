A <Term id="prompti">prompt</Term> is the instruction you give Claude. A good prompt says three things:

1. **What?** What you want to achieve.
2. **Why?** For what purpose or for whom.
3. **Within which limits?** What it may and may not do.

Claude can’t see your thoughts. The more it knows about the goal, the less it needs to guess.

<NuppuSays pose="keksii">Imagine you’re giving instructions to a new coworker who doesn’t know your project. What would they need to know?</NuppuSays>

## Bad and good side by side

### A new page

- **Bad:** “Make a page.”
- **Good:** “Make a simple website that presents my crafting hobby. The page has a heading, a short introduction and a gallery of three pictures. I’ll add the pictures myself later. The readers are my friends, so the tone can be relaxed. Use only HTML and CSS.”

### Fixing a bug

- **Bad:** “Fix this.”
- **Good:** “When I press the Send button, nothing happens. I expected a thank-you message to appear. Find out why and fix it, but don’t change the page’s design.”

### Design

- **Bad:** “Improve the design.”
- **Good:** “Make the page clearer on a phone: the text should be easy to read and the buttons big enough for a finger. Keep the current colors.”

## More tips

- **One thing at a time.** It’s worth breaking a big request into pieces.
- **Say what happened.** When something goes wrong, paste in the error message.
- **Ask it to ask.** “If anything is unclear, ask before you start.”
- **Ask it to explain.** “Briefly explain what you changed and why.” That way you learn as you go.

<Path only="claude-code-desktop">

- **Show it with a picture.** Attach a screenshot to your message, for example of an error or of a site you like: click the **+** button next to the text box or drag the picture into the box.

</Path>

Claude understands many languages very well, so write in the language that’s most natural for you. You don’t need to translate technical words. If an error message is in English, paste it in as it is.
